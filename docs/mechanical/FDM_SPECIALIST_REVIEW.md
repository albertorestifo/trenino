# FDM specialist review — integrated controller, 24 October 2026

Review basis: `integrated_controller.fs`, all 30 exported STL objects and the mesh/slicer reports in `integrated-print`, and the assembly/print guide. This is an independent design-for-FDM review of the current revision, before the requested main-gear/handle and 2020-frame redesign. Dimensions below are taken directly from the FeatureScript. No physical print or load test has been performed.

## Findings to implement before releasing prints

1. **Fork is not lying on the flat side claimed by the print layout.** Bars are y=-52…-44, but the roller ends now have radius 5 about y=-48, extending to y=-53. After the +90 degree X print rotation, the cylindrical nose sets Z=0 and the full long bars start 1 mm above the bed. The first contact is a rounded tangent, not a stable broad face. Make the fork bars 12 mm wide (y=-54…-42) with a common flat lower side at y=-54. A local radius-6 pivot boss can fit inside this width. Keep the radius-5 roller ends inside this envelope. Re-run cam and support clearances after widening. Otherwise the part requires extensive underside support, with poorer bearing-hole accuracy.
2. **Fork pivot ligaments are too thin for a repeat-loaded printed part.** The Ø5.3 hole crosses 8 mm wide bars, leaving only (8-5.3)/2 = 1.35 mm of material each side. The 12 mm width above raises that to 3.35 mm. Use rounded transitions into the bar rather than sharp notches. This is a recommended reinforcement, not a calculated load rating.
3. **Roller-axle head seat is only 1.2 mm thick.** Upper cheek z42…45 is cut by the counterbore beginning at z43.2. A clamped screw can crush or split this thin seat. Provide at least 2 mm solid thickness below the head counterbore, preferably by thickening the roller-end cheek locally; preserve the internal 12 mm tube spacing and recheck head clearance and the cam-removal route. Do not solve it simply by pulling the two fork cheeks together with the axle screw.
4. **The shaft needs explicit support under its journals.** D-flat-down is the correct starting orientation for a printed torque shaft, but its 9.2 mm journals are 0.4 mm above the bed when the D-flat establishes Z=0. Add local removable support under both journals or sacrificial print-only feet. Do not remove journal geometry to create flat bearing surfaces. Keep support away from deep insert bores and clean the running surfaces afterward.
5. **No integrated slicing success has yet been demonstrated.** The current `slice-report.json` records exit -3 and zero-byte outputs for every part. Logs say the settings file `integrated-print/profiles/process-integrated*.json` is missing; the generated process files are actually in `integrated-print/`. Correct the path before interpreting any result as a geometry/slicing failure. The shaft is also marked `Supports=false` in the current report. Re-slice the final revised exports, not these superseded meshes.

## Release checks for the revised design

- Regenerate the 2020-supported revision and all print groups before exporting. The current 30-part meshes pass topology and build-envelope checks, but that does not cover revised geometry or physical strength.
- Use a first fit job with shaft, bushings, a short insert boss coupon and the roller. Confirm heat-set insert installation and both rotating fits before printing large plates or gear/handle.
- Start with 0.20 mm layers, five walls and five top/bottom layers. Five walls matter more than merely raising infill for these small bosses; use local solid infill around bearing/insert seats if the slicer leaves sparse cores. 35% gyroid is a reasonable prototype starting point, not a strength qualification.
- PLA is suitable for initial fit/geometry evaluation. PETG changes compliance and fits; recheck those after switching. Avoid treating either material as validated for stop impacts until a modest-load bench test has been completed.
- Re-run full lever clearance and cam-removal checks after the fork width, head-seat and gear/handle revisions. The added 2020 rail geometry must be included in those checks.
- Verify support accessibility and layer preview, including the first five shaft/fork layers. A watertight mesh and a zero-interference CAD sweep alone do not prove printability.

## Requested implementation fragment

`review_fork_fragment.fs` implements the agreed candidate: 14 mm bars sharing y=-55 flat print side, Ø7.3 fork pivot bore over a 7 OD × 5.2 ID × 20 mm metal sleeve, 4 mm upper cheek with 2.2 mm remaining axle head seat, 9.5 mm-wide roller, and 11 mm axle spacer tube. The main agent must integrate and collision-test it. The stationary pivot sleeve must not be added to the rotating `rollerHardware` query. Its ends clamp against the fixed plate faces at z26 and z46; there remains 1 mm axial clearance to each outer fork face. The rounded roller nose beyond x=0 still needs a small accessible support region in this orientation; the long bars now have broad bed contact.
