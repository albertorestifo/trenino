# Independent mechanical review — integrated controller

Reviewed 24 October 2026 against `integrated_controller.fs`, the exported original `Full device` assembly (`tmp/cam_detent/original-full-device.3mf`), and the integrated print guide. This review is independent of the original implementation. It reviews geometry and load paths, not measured material strength or a physical prototype.

## Required changes before releasing the revised print package

1. **Use the actual lever-to-main-gear connection and a continuous torque path.** The original assembly's Fastened 2 connects Handle to Main Gear, the 48T driver. The current separate z41–51 D-hub lever is not that arrangement. Integrate a broad, filleted lever root into the main gear at its original radial attachment. The small 24T potentiometer gear should remain an instrument drive, not carry hand or end-stop loads. Put the cam/stop torque through a positively keyed or clamped carrier into the main gear/shaft; the current 12mm D shaft in a 12.4mm D socket permits noticeable clearance/backlash. Do not rely on a radial set screw bearing against printed plastic for all torque transfer.

2. **Rebuild axial retention when relocating the handle.** Current upper spacer is z51–53.5 because the handle hub occupies z41–51. Removing the handle hub leaves about10mm of uncontrolled axial space. Replace it with a continuous spacer or positive collar/shoulder that captures the carrier independently of the removable cam and handle. Maintain a small measurable running end float, not interference against the frame. The shaft end caps presently tighten onto the shaft ends rather than deliberately clamping the bushings; preserve that distinction.

3. **Model and use the real 2020 frame.** The original full-assembly component transforms differ from the Part Studio placement. The two plates are opposite-handed in the assembly; simply importing the Part Studio bodies does not reproduce their mounted spacing. Preserve their mounting interfaces, explicitly set their separation along the longitudinal T-slots, and show both in the full 2020 rig. Mount the fixed detent cassette and stop structure to extrusion-backed brackets. Prefer two separated T-nuts per bracket, or a locating tongue plus a second fastener, so a single screw cannot allow bracket rotation. Do not depend on friction alone in a long printed cantilever. Use metal washers and the actual selected T-nut/bolt size and show tool access. Side-load support and end-stop loads should close into extrusion, not into the potentiometer body.

4. **Strengthen the end-stop contact.** Current carrier lug is only6mm wide ×4mm axial thickness (z28–32), starts at y22, and reaches y37 with its rounded tip. It has an abrupt root and strikes a Ø7 post protruding5mm above a6mm plate. A zero-interference sweep does not validate impact strength. Widen the root to at least10–12mm where the neighboring geometry permits, use substantial fillets/gussets, and increase overlap/contact area. Prefer a metal bolt/dowel stop with a replaceable polymer contact pad supported by the extrusion-backed bracket. Keep mechanical stops independent of the potentiometer's internal stops. These dimensions are prototype starting points, not a strength certification.

5. **Make the spring adjuster a buildable set of parts.** The modeled M4 adjuster is a rod y−39…−15 with an integral Ø9 thrust disk y−30…−28, not a specified purchasable screw assembly. Define an outside-accessible M4 screw, captured nut, and separate guided thrust cup/washer. Add a shallow Ø8.4-ish locating seat or a suitable guide peg at the fork tail so the Ø8 spring cannot walk sideways off the flat bridge. Ensure the cup remains captured at minimum preload and cannot bottom at maximum compression. Model the real screw head, nut and access path.

6. **Use a smooth follower pivot bearing surface.** A nominal M5×30 button screw is currently represented by a smooth cylinder through Ø5.3 printed fork bores. A normal fully threaded screw does not provide that bearing surface in reality. Use a shoulder bolt or an explicit smooth sleeve/bushing and re-size the fork bore. Clamp a fixed-distance sleeve between the support plates so tightening the nut cannot squeeze the rotating fork. Maintain small axial washers/clearances on each side. The existing double-supported geometry is preferable to the abandoned cantilever, but the actual hardware must match it.

## Additional findings and exact checks

- **Gear travel:**48:24 ratio means140° lever travel drives280° of potentiometer rotation. Verify the actual device's permitted mechanical/electrical travel and centering. Parameterize hard stops to the chosen potentiometer; do not assume the CAD body proves that280° is acceptable. The original 3MF contains its actual body/shaft envelope, so include it in final mounting and clearance checks rather than a generic Ø6 cylinder.
- **Cam gate positions:**`pitch=140°/count` with odd counts puts the ±70° hard stops at cam crests, not valleys. For3 gates the valley positions are about−46.7°,0°,46.7°; for7 gates they are−60° through+60° in20° increments. This is internally consistent only if the ends are intentionally half a gate beyond the outer detents. If the user expects a firm outer gate at each limit, use endpoint-aware spacing, e.g.140°/(count−1), and rederive/validate the profile and stop angle.
- **Spring sizing:**The36mm roller arm and18mm spring tail require roughly twice the roller normal force at the spring. At1.5mm cam lift the spring compresses only about0.75mm extra. For a129mm lever, ideal peak hand-force/spring-force ratios are approximately0.022,0.037,0.052 for3/5/7 gates; the same spring therefore gives noticeably different feel across profiles. Select preload/rate from a stated hand-force target and spring allowable stroke. Friction and compliance change these estimates.
- **Pressure angle:**Numerical evaluation of the source center path at maximum1.5mm lift gives peak contact-normal/follower-motion angles approximately8.0°,12.4°,16.6° for3/5/7 layouts. The profile itself has no obvious severe pressure-angle problem. This does not prove contact durability, exact fitted-spline curvature, or absence of wear.
- **Cam retention:**The three M3×8 cam screws have about5.5mm engagement into the5.7mm inserts with the modeled seats. Carrier insert bosses Ø9 around Ø4 pockets provide2.5mm nominal radial material before insertion. These are reasonable prototype proportions. Keep asymmetric index location and avoid relying solely on screw clearance for indexing.
- **Other insert engagement:**Lower spring screws reach approximately5.5mm into their inserts; upper spring screws likewise. Rear and front long mounting screws extend past some inserts into the existing Ø3.4 through bores, so they are not automatically bottomed by the pocket floor. Check actual screw lengths, insert seating and washer thickness after printing.
- **Roller axle:**The Ø4×Ø3.2×12mm sleeve clamped between the fork cheeks and10mm-wide roller with Ø4.3 bore is a mechanically coherent arrangement. It leaves1mm axial clearance each end. The M3×20 axle has about0.9mm tip projection past the modeled2.4mm nut; verify with the selected real nut. The top head recess leaves only1.2mm of local cheek thickness below it, so use a generous end boss and local solid perimeters, and avoid excessive tightening.
- **Printed shaft:**The Ø12 D working shaft and Ø9.2 journals are more plausible than a small unsupported printed rod. However its abrupt shoulder transitions need generous radii compatible with the bushing lead-ins. Retain D-flat-down printing. Do not claim strength merely from fit or a collision sweep. Test modest hand loads and end-stop contact first.
- **Bushings:**Ø15.9 bushings in nominalØ16.1 seats are slip fits; add a flange/capture to control migration and make bearing replacement deliberate. Ø9.6 bore onØ9.2 journal gives0.4mm diametral clearance, potentially perceptible at the handle. Validate a fit coupon and reduce clearance if the printer/material supports it; avoid promising zero play from nominal CAD.
- **Service:**Keep the removable sector screws accessible with the rail brackets installed. Re-run the removal path after moving the handle to the gear and after adding extrusion hardware; the previous path proves only the superseded stack. Include real fastener heads, cups, spring, pot body, and rails in the final checks.

## Verification required after implementation

Regenerate the actual user Part Studio (not just the test endpoint), verify axial capture and every hardware stack, inspect the entire assembled rig, sweep the permitted lever range for all cam profiles, confirm intended endpoint gate behavior, test sector-removal/tool-access paths, and repeat mesh/orientation/slicer checks for changed printable parts. Physical spring-force, backlash, wear, shaft-root and stop-impact tests remain prototype validation.
